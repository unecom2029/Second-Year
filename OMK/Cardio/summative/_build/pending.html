🔑 Where the file stands

All eight Parts (Sections 1–37) are written. 100 of the 101 study-guide objectives are covered in full. The exception is objective 15 (the echo predictive value for a newborn murmur), where no lecture gives the number. Section 37 (women’s cardiovascular health) is supporting material with no objective of its own. The coverage map shows each objective’s status.

Part A · things to know

Where the sources disagree

  • Atrial kick: Dr. Qazi said 20–25% of output; Dr. Morganelli said about 10% of end-diastolic volume; the electrophysiologist said about 15% (Sections 1 and 9).
  • c wave timing: the recording placed it in rapid ejection; boards place it at the onset of systole (Section 1).
  • S4 timing: the recording said “before S2”; it is before S1 (Section 2).
  • Pulse pressure in aortic stenosis: the lecture said it varies; UWorld says narrow in severe disease (Section 3).
  • Renin and ADH directions: two slips in the physiology recording (Section 5).
Part B · things to know

Slips and judgement calls

  • Carotid body versus carotid sinus: the arrhythmia recording says the carotid body carries parasympathetic tone. Carotid massage works on the carotid sinus baroreceptors; the body is a chemoreceptor (Section 8).
  • Adenosine and cyclic AMP: the recording says adenosine works through an “uptick in cAMP.” The A1 receptor is Gi-coupled, so cyclic AMP falls and potassium efflux rises (Sections 8 and 10).
  • QT versus QRS: in the long-QT case the recording says “QRS” once where it means QT (Section 8).
  • Wellens’ morphology table was explicitly excluded by the lecturer (“I do not expect you to know this, nor would it be tested”), so Section 8 gives the four Brugada steps and stops there.
  • Delayed- versus rapid delayed-rectifier potassium subtypes were explicitly excluded by Dr. Scully, so Section 10 names IKr for dofetilide and does not go further.
  • No UWorld figures were used. Two useful ones from the lecture notes — the conduction-system drawing and the coronary dominance diagram — are UWorld images, so they were left out and the anatomy is given as text instead.
Part C · things to know

Where the sources disagree

  • Wavy fibres: the lecture slide puts them at ½–4 hours, First Aid at 0–4 hours, UWorld at 4–12 hours. All agree they are the first light-microscope change, on day 1 (Section 14).
  • Free-wall rupture timing: Dr. Gardner 3–7 days; Dr. Watkins two peaks (24 hours and 3–5 days); First Aid 5–14 days; UWorld up to 2 weeks (Section 15).
  • The papillary-rupture murmur: the case slide says holosystolic to the axilla; UWorld says often early systolic and silent in up to half (Section 15).
  • Ivabradine’s mechanism: one Application Session 1 slide gives ranolazine’s (late sodium current). Ivabradine blocks the funny current (Section 16).
  • The acute coronary syndrome bundle was left by Dr. Scully to the clinical lectures, so Section 16 builds it from Dr. Watkins’s case plus the UWorld library, with the library material tagged.
Part D · things to know

Slips and judgement calls

  • HFpEF and the ejection fraction: the physiology recording says “reduced ejection fraction” at the end of the HFpEF explanation; the slide is right that it is preserved (Section 17).
  • Vericiguat is called an sGC “inhibitor” on one slide; it is a stimulator (Section 18).
  • Intercalated cells: the recording says “beta”; hydrogen-ion secretion is by α-intercalated cells (Section 18).
  • “Most common cardiac tumour”: the lecturers mean primary; metastases are the most common overall (Section 20).
  • ARVC, takotsubo, rhabdomyoma and the echo features of each cardiomyopathy are thin in the lectures, so they are built from First Aid, the ECG II and Women’s Health notes and AMBOSS, and tagged.
Part E · things to know

Where the sources disagree

  • Endocarditis incidence: the lecture says about 1–3 per 100,000; the outline says 3–7 (Section 21).
  • Leukopenia: the lecturer said it is not usual; the outline lists it (Section 22).
  • MSSA duration: the handout’s “3–5 days” is almost certainly a typo for about 6 weeks (Section 22).
  • Bartonella: corrected in class to doxycycline plus rifampin, not gentamicin (Section 22).
  • The IDU valve: the board answer is tricuspid; the pediatric case showed a mitral vegetation (Section 21).
  • Antibiotic mechanisms, surgical indications and dental prophylaxis are not in the lecture; Section 22 gives the First Aid and AMBOSS versions, tagged.
Part F · things to know

Slips and judgement calls

  • TAVR durability: slide 58 says 50% degeneration at 8 years; slide 59 and the lecture say current valves track like surgical ones to about 9 years (Section 25).
  • The AS pulse: the surgery recording first calls it “water hammer” and then corrects itself — AS is pulsus parvus et tardus; water hammer is AR.
  • The AI murmur: one slide says crescendo–decrescendo diastolic to the axilla; the board version is early diastolic decrescendo at the left sternal border (Section 3).
  • Right-sided valves and TAVR complications beyond heart block and coronary obstruction come from the UWorld library, tagged. No calcific aortic stenosis gross photograph was available outside UWorld, so the Qazi schematic stands in.
Part G · partly covered

Objective 15: the echo predictive value

  • What is missing: the lecture gives the incidence chart and says a persistent newborn murmur needs echo, but it gives no figure for the predictive value of echo (or how often a newborn murmur is structural). Section 27 offers an approximate range from the literature, flagged as such. If the new study guide or a lecture slide gives a number, put it in Section 27 and mark objective 15 covered.
  • Incidence figures differ: VSD about 31% on the pediatric chart and 42% on Dr. Gardner’s table; one slide’s “2–5% of births” should read 2–5 per 1,000.
  • Recording slips in the pediatric lecture (the HLHS flow directions and which artery leaves which ventricle in transposition) are corrected in the source notes and in Section 26.
Part H · things to know

Where the sources disagree

  • How fast vascular claudication settles: one App Session 2 table gives 30 minutes for vascular and 1–3 minutes for neurogenic pain. The guideline tables on the same slides say the reverse: vascular pain settles within minutes of standing still (Section 35).
  • “Anticoagulation first” for claudication: a recording slip in the surgery lecture. Claudication gets antiplatelet therapy; IV heparin is the first step only in acute limb ischemia (Section 35).
  • Women’s cardiovascular health (Section 37) has no summative objective, so it is kept short, and the female-specific presentations (SCAD, Takotsubo, MINOCA) are board add-ons.
  • Pictures: UWorld figures for the renin–angiotensin system, diuretic sites, the thiazide–calcium link, pre-eclampsia and the lipid-drug sites were left out. Lecture slides and AMBOSS images stand in.
