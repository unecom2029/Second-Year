The cardiac cycle and pressure–volume loops

a, c and v waves
The three atrial pressure waves: atrial contraction, the atrioventricular valve bulging back at the onset of systole, and atrial filling against the closed valve.
Afterload
The load the ventricle must overcome before it can eject — mainly aortic diastolic pressure.
Atrial kick
The last part of ventricular filling, supplied by atrial contraction. Lost in atrial fibrillation.
CO — cardiac output
Stroke volume times heart rate; about 4–5 L/min at rest. The cardiac index is cardiac output divided by body surface area.
Diastasis
The slow middle part of ventricular filling, between rapid filling and the atrial kick. The first part of diastole to disappear when the heart rate rises.
Dicrotic notch
The small dip on the aortic pressure tracing at aortic valve closure.
EDV / ESV — end-diastolic / end-systolic volume
How full the ventricle is just before it contracts, and what is left after it ejects.
EDPVR — end-diastolic pressure–volume relationship
The passive filling curve on a PV loop. Steeper means a stiffer, less compliant ventricle.
EF — ejection fraction
Stroke volume divided by EDV; normally 55–70%.
ESPVR — end-systolic pressure–volume relationship
The line joining the end-systolic corners of loops at different fillings. Its slope is contractility.
Isovolumetric (isovolumic)
A phase with all four valves closed, so volume stays constant while pressure rises (contraction) or falls (relaxation).
Preload
Stretch on the ventricle before it contracts, indexed by EDV or end-diastolic pressure.
PV loop — pressure–volume loop
Left ventricular pressure plotted against volume for one beat.
SV — stroke volume
EDV minus ESV; about 70 mL.
Wiggers diagram
Aortic, ventricular and atrial pressures, ventricular volume, the ECG and the heart sounds on one time axis.

Heart sounds and murmurs

A2 / P2
The aortic and pulmonic components of the second heart sound.
AR / AS — aortic regurgitation / stenosis
A leaking aortic valve (diastolic murmur, wide pulse pressure) / a narrowed one (systolic murmur, gradient across the valve).
Austin Flint murmur
An apical diastolic rumble in severe aortic regurgitation that mimics mitral stenosis.
Concentric / eccentric hypertrophy
A thick wall from pressure overload / a dilated chamber from volume overload.
HCM — hypertrophic cardiomyopathy
A thick septum that obstructs outflow; its murmur gets louder when the ventricle is smaller.
LBBB / RBBB — left / right bundle branch block
Delayed conduction down one bundle branch; LBBB can give paradoxical splitting, RBBB wide splitting.
MR / MS — mitral regurgitation / stenosis
A leaking mitral valve (tall v wave, holosystolic murmur) / a narrowed one (diastolic gradient, rumble and opening snap).
MVP — mitral valve prolapse
Floppy, myxomatous leaflets that billow into the atrium; midsystolic click and late systolic murmur.
Opening snap
The sound of a stiff mitral valve snapping open in early diastole; a shorter S2-to-snap interval means more severe stenosis.
Pulsus parvus et tardus
A weak, late-peaking pulse — severe aortic stenosis.
S1–S4
Atrioventricular valve closure · semilunar valve closure · early-diastolic volume sound · late-diastolic stiff-ventricle sound.
Valsalva maneuver
Forced expiration against a closed glottis; the strain phase lowers venous return and preload.

Exercise and prevention

ASCVD — atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease
Coronary disease, stroke and peripheral artery disease caused by atherosclerosis.
CR — cardiac rehabilitation
A supervised secondary-prevention programme of exercise, education and risk-factor management. Phase II is 36 ECG-monitored sessions.
DASH — Dietary Approaches to Stop Hypertension
A diet rich in fruit, vegetables and low-fat dairy that lowers blood pressure, more so with less sodium.
FITT
Frequency, intensity, time and type — the parts of an exercise prescription.
HF-ACTION / REHAB-HF
The heart-failure exercise trials: HF-ACTION in reduced ejection fraction, REHAB-HF in frail older patients after an admission.
Heart-rate reserve
Peak heart rate minus resting heart rate.
MET — metabolic equivalent
The energy of resting metabolism, about 3.5 mL of oxygen per kg per minute.
PREDIMED
The Spanish trial showing that a Mediterranean diet with olive oil or nuts cut cardiovascular events by about 30%.
RPE — rating of perceived exertion
The Borg scale, 6–20; training is usually prescribed at 11–16.
VO₂max / VO₂peak
Oxygen uptake at maximal effort / at the point symptoms stop the test.

Receptors, signals and control of the circulation

ACE — angiotensin-converting enzyme
Converts angiotensin I to II and, as kininase II, breaks down bradykinin.
ADH — antidiuretic hormone (vasopressin)
Makes the collecting duct reabsorb water without sodium.
Bainbridge reflex
A rise in heart rate when the atria are stretched by extra volume.
CVP — central venous pressure
Pressure in the vena cava near the right atrium; the clinical stand-in for right atrial pressure.
Funny current (If)
The slow inward sodium current of pacemaker phase 4, switched on by hyperpolarization; its slope sets the heart rate.
Gq / Gi / Gs
The three G-protein pathways: Gq raises calcium through IP3, Gi lowers cAMP, Gs raises cAMP.
MAP — mean arterial pressure
The average arterial pressure over a cycle, about 90–95 mm Hg; equals cardiac output times total peripheral resistance.
MLCK — myosin light-chain kinase
The enzyme that switches smooth muscle contraction on; PKA inhibits it.
Pmsf — mean systemic filling pressure
The pressure throughout the circulation when flow stops, about 7–8 mm Hg, set by the stressed volume.
PDE3 / PDE5 — phosphodiesterase 3 / 5
Enzymes that break down cAMP (blocked by milrinone and inamrinone) and cGMP (blocked by sildenafil).
RAAS — renin–angiotensin–aldosterone system
The kidney’s long-term pressure and volume defence.
Stressed / unstressed volume
Blood that stretches the vein walls and makes pressure / blood that fills them without making pressure.
TPR / SVR — total peripheral / systemic vascular resistance
The resistance of the whole systemic circulation, set mostly by arteriolar radius.

The ECG and arrhythmias

Aberrancy
A supraventricular beat conducted down the ventricles with a bundle-branch pattern, so it looks wide. The reason a wide tachycardia is not automatically ventricular.
ARVC — arrhythmogenic right ventricular cardiomyopathy
An inherited disease in which the right ventricular wall is replaced by fibro-fatty tissue, predisposing to ventricular arrhythmia.
AV dissociation — atrioventricular dissociation
Atria and ventricles depolarizing on independent schedules. Seen in complete heart block and in ventricular tachycardia.
AVNRT — atrioventricular nodal re-entrant tachycardia
Re-entry between a slow and a fast pathway inside the atrioventricular node. The P wave hides in the QRS.
AVRT — atrioventricular re-entrant tachycardia
Re-entry using an accessory pathway as one limb. Orthodromic (down the node, up the pathway) is narrow; antidromic is wide.
Bazett’s formula
The corrected QT equals the measured QT divided by the square root of the R–R interval in seconds.
Brugada syndrome
An inherited sodium-channel disease of the right ventricular outflow tract, giving coved ST elevation in V1 and V2 and a risk of sudden death. Not the same as the Brugada algorithm for wide tachycardias.
Bundle of Kent
The accessory pathway of Wolff–Parkinson–White, connecting atrium to ventricle directly and bypassing the atrioventricular node.
Capture beat
A single narrow beat during a wide tachycardia, where a sinus impulse got through the node. Nearly diagnostic of ventricular tachycardia. A fusion beat is the hybrid version.
CHA₂DS₂-VASc / CHA₂DS₂-VA
The stroke-risk score in atrial fibrillation, with and without the point for female sex.
Cinchonism
Quinidine toxicity: reduced hearing, tinnitus, blurred vision and delirium. Named after the cinchona bark the drug comes from.
Concordance (precordial)
Every QRS from V1 to V6 pointing the same way. Negative concordance is virtually diagnostic of ventricular tachycardia.
CTI — cavotricuspid isthmus
The strip of right atrial tissue between the inferior vena cava and the tricuspid annulus. The circuit of typical flutter runs through it, and it is the ablation target.
Delta wave
The slurred upstroke of a pre-excited QRS, caused by early ventricular activation through an accessory pathway. It eats into the PR segment, so the PR is short.
EAD / DAD — early / delayed afterdepolarization
Triggered activity. Early ones occur during a prolonged repolarization and cause torsades; delayed ones occur after repolarization from calcium overload and cause ischemic and digoxin arrhythmias.
Early repolarization
J-point elevation from a larger transient outward potassium current in the epicardium than the endocardium. Usually a normal variant in young people.
Ebstein anomaly
A tricuspid valve displaced downward into the right ventricle. The congenital lesion most strongly associated with Wolff–Parkinson–White.
Electromechanical dissociation
Electrical activity with no effective contraction. The feared result of giving intravenous verapamil to a patient on a beta-blocker.
ERP — effective refractory period
The interval during which a cell cannot be re-excited. Lengthening it is how class III drugs stop re-entry.
Funny current (If)
The sodium-carried pacemaker current that produces the slow phase 4 drift. “Funny” because hyperpolarization, not depolarization, opens it.
Holiday heart
Atrial fibrillation precipitated by an alcohol binge.
Mobitz I (Wenckebach) / Mobitz II
The two forms of second-degree block. Mobitz I lengthens the PR until a beat drops and is benign; Mobitz II drops a beat with a constant PR and needs a pacemaker.
NAPA — N-acetylprocainamide
The active metabolite of procainamide, formed by hepatic acetylation and cleared by the kidney.
Northwest axis
An axis pointing up and to the right, so aVR is strikingly positive. A feature favouring ventricular tachycardia.
P mitrale / P pulmonale
A wide notched P wave of left atrial enlargement / a tall peaked P wave of right atrial enlargement.
Pre-excitation
Ventricular activation that begins through an accessory pathway before the atrioventricular node delivers the impulse. Gives a short PR and a delta wave.
P1 purinergic receptor
The adenosine receptor. Acting through Gi/o, it opens potassium channels and hyperpolarizes nodal tissue.
R-wave progression
The growth of the R wave from V1 to V6, with the transition where R equals S normally at V3 to V4.
Re-entry
A self-sustaining circuit between two pathways of different conduction speed and refractoriness. The commonest arrhythmia mechanism.
RVR — rapid ventricular response
Atrial fibrillation conducting fast to the ventricles.
Schwartz score
A diagnostic score for long QT syndrome combining the tracing, the clinical history and the family history. Four or more means high probability.
Sokolow–Lyon criterion
The S wave in V1 or V2 plus the R wave in V5 or V6 totalling 35 mm or more — the most sensitive voltage criterion for left ventricular hypertrophy.
Strain pattern
T-wave inversion and ST depression accompanying ventricular hypertrophy, easily mistaken for ischemia.
TdP — torsades de pointes
Polymorphic ventricular tachycardia with a twisting axis, arising on a long QT. Treated acutely with intravenous magnesium.
Use-dependence
More drug block at faster heart rates, because the drug binds the open and inactivated channel states. A property of class I drugs.
WAP / MAT — wandering atrial pacemaker / multifocal atrial tachycardia
Three or more P-wave shapes with an irregular rhythm, below 100 beats per minute and at 100 or above respectively.
Xanthopsia
Yellow-green colour vision with halos around lights — a sign of digoxin toxicity.

Atherosclerosis and ischemic heart disease

ACS — acute coronary syndrome
Unstable angina, NSTEMI or STEMI: plaque rupture with thrombus.
ALDH2 — mitochondrial aldehyde dehydrogenase-2
The enzyme that converts organic nitrates to nitric oxide. Polymorphisms common in East Asian populations blunt nitrate effect.
Arteriosclerosis / atherosclerosis
Any hardening of the arteries / the subset caused by intimal lipid plaques (atheromas).
BNP / NT-proBNP — B-type natriuretic peptide / its N-terminal fragment
Released in equal amounts from stretched ventricular myocytes. BNP is active and short-lived; NT-proBNP is inert, longer-lived and renally cleared.
CAC — coronary artery calcium score
A CT measure of calcified plaque; a score of zero makes obstructive disease much less likely.
CCTA — coronary computed tomography angiography
Anatomic, non-invasive imaging of the coronary lumen.
CK-MB — creatine kinase, MB fraction
An older cardiac marker that clears in 2–3 days, so it can show re-infarction.
Contraction band necrosis
Hypereosinophilic transverse bands in myocytes from calcium influx on reperfusion.
Coronary flow reserve
How much coronary flow can rise above rest. Falls from about 50% stenosis, whereas resting flow holds until about 80–90%.
Coronary steal
Diversion of flow away from an ischemic bed when a strong arteriolar dilator opens healthy beds.
Dressler syndrome
Autoimmune (post-cardiac injury) pericarditis weeks after an infarct.
Fatty streak
The earliest visible atherosclerotic lesion: collections of lipid-laden foam cells in the intima.
FMD — fibromuscular dysplasia
Segmental replacement of the arterial wall by connective tissue, giving a “string of beads” angiogram; classically the renal arteries of young women.
Foam cell
A macrophage (or smooth muscle cell) filled with oxidized LDL.
GRACE score
Global Registry of Acute Coronary Events: a risk score that helps time angiography in NSTE-ACS.
hs-cTn — high-sensitivity cardiac troponin
A troponin assay able to detect very small rises, allowing rule-in or rule-out at 0 and 1–3 hours.
Hyaline / hyperplastic arteriolosclerosis
Glassy pink arteriolar wall thickening (benign hypertension, diabetes) / concentric “onion-skin” layers (malignant hypertension).
Ivabradine
A blocker of the funny current (If) through HCN channels in the sinus node, slowing the heart rate.
MINOCA / INOCA
Myocardial infarction / ischemia with non-obstructive coronary arteries.
Mönckeberg medial calcific sclerosis
Calcification of the arterial media with an open lumen; usually asymptomatic.
Nitrate-free interval
A daily break from long-acting nitrates, usually overnight, to prevent tolerance.
NSTE-ACS
Non-ST-elevation acute coronary syndrome: unstable angina or NSTEMI.
PDE5 — phosphodiesterase-5
The enzyme that breaks down cGMP; inhibited by sildenafil and tadalafil.
PEA — pulseless electrical activity
An organised rhythm on the monitor with no pulse; after an infarct, think free-wall rupture with tamponade.
Pseudoaneurysm
A contained ventricular rupture walled only by pericardium and clot; high risk of rupture.
Ranolazine
Blocks the late sodium current in ischemic myocytes, lowering calcium overload and diastolic wall stress.
Type 1 / type 2 MI (primary / secondary)
Infarction from plaque rupture with thrombus / from a supply–demand mismatch such as sepsis or tachycardia.
Vasospastic (Prinzmetal, variant) angina
Rest angina from coronary spasm, with transient ST elevation.

Heart failure, cardiomyopathy and tumours

ACC/AHA stages A–D
At risk; structural disease without symptoms; symptomatic; refractory heart failure.
ARNI — angiotensin receptor–neprilysin inhibitor
Sacubitril/valsartan: blocks neprilysin (raising natriuretic peptides) and the AT1 receptor.
ARVC — arrhythmogenic right ventricular cardiomyopathy
Inherited fibro-fatty replacement of the right ventricle causing ventricular arrhythmias.
Braking phenomenon
Diuretic tolerance as the distal tubule hypertrophies and reabsorbs more sodium.
Carney complex
A familial syndrome of multiple myxomas, spotty skin pigmentation and endocrine tumours.
Concentric / eccentric hypertrophy
Thick wall with a normal cavity from pressure overload / dilated cavity from volume overload.
Forrester profiles
The bedside grid of congestion (wet or dry) against perfusion (warm or cold) in acute heart failure.
GDMT — guideline-directed medical therapy
The four mortality-reducing heart-failure drug classes, titrated to target doses.
H₂FPEF score
A 0–9 score of obesity, hypertension, AF, pulmonary hypertension, age and filling pressure estimating the probability of HFpEF.
Heart-failure cells
Hemosiderin-laden alveolar macrophages in chronic left heart failure.
HFrEF / HFmrEF / HFpEF / HFimpEF
Heart failure with EF ≤ 40% / 41–49% / ≥ 50% / previously ≤ 40% and now above 40%.
HOCM — hypertrophic obstructive cardiomyopathy
HCM with outflow-tract obstruction from the septal bulge and systolic anterior motion of the mitral valve.
MRA — mineralocorticoid receptor antagonist
Spironolactone, eplerenone, finerenone: block the aldosterone receptor.
Myofibre disarray
Hypertrophied myocytes in a haphazard, whorled pattern — the histology of hypertrophic cardiomyopathy.
Neprilysin
A neutral endopeptidase that degrades natriuretic peptides, bradykinin and substance P.
Nutmeg liver
Mottled centrilobular congestion from right heart failure.
NYHA classes I–IV
Symptom classes from no limitation to symptoms at rest.
SAM — systolic anterior motion
The mitral leaflet pulled toward the septum in systole, obstructing the outflow tract in HOCM.
SGLT2i — sodium–glucose cotransporter-2 inhibitor
Dapagliflozin, empagliflozin: natriuretic drugs that lower mortality in HFrEF and HFpEF.
Takotsubo cardiomyopathy
Stress-induced transient apical ballooning, typically in postmenopausal women.
Titin
The giant elastic sarcomere protein; a shift to its stiff N2B isoform contributes to HFpEF.
Vericiguat
A soluble guanylate cyclase stimulator used as an add-on in worsening HFrEF.

Infection and inflammation of the heart

Beck triad
Hypotension, distended neck veins and muffled heart sounds — cardiac tamponade.
Biofilm
A matrix-embedded community of slow-growing bacteria on a surface, far more resistant to antibiotics; made by S. aureus and S. epidermidis.
CAR — coxsackie–adenovirus receptor
The myocyte receptor shared by Coxsackie B and adenovirus.
Chagas disease
Infection with Trypanosoma cruzi from the triatomine bug; decades later cardiomyopathy, megaesophagus and megacolon.
CoNS — coagulase-negative staphylococci
Chiefly S. epidermidis; the organism of prosthetic-valve and device endocarditis.
Constrictive pericarditis
A scarred, stiff pericardium limiting filling; Kussmaul sign and a pericardial knock.
Duke criteria (2023)
Major and minor criteria for endocarditis; definite with 2 major, 1 major + 3 minor, or 5 minor.
EMB — endomyocardial biopsy
A catheter biopsy of the right ventricular septum; the definitive test for myocarditis.
HACEK
Haemophilus, Aggregatibacter, Cardiobacterium, Eikenella, Kingella: slow-growing gram-negative endocarditis organisms.
Janeway lesions / Osler nodes
Painless embolic macules on palms and soles / painful immune-complex nodules on finger and toe pads.
Libman–Sacks endocarditis
Sterile flat vegetations on both sides of the valve leaflets in SLE.
Marantic (non-bacterial thrombotic) endocarditis
Sterile platelet–fibrin vegetations in advanced cancer or other hypercoagulable states.
Mycotic aneurysm
An arterial aneurysm caused by an infected (septic) embolus weakening the wall.
Myopericarditis / perimyocarditis
Pericarditis with myocardial involvement and normal function / myocarditis with pericardial features and reduced function.
Pericardial window
A surgical opening (usually subxiphoid) that drains a pericardial effusion into the abdomen or pleura.
Pulsus paradoxus
An inspiratory fall in systolic pressure of more than 10 mm Hg; a sign of tamponade.
Roth spots
Retinal hemorrhages with pale centres, from immune complexes in endocarditis.
Septic pulmonary emboli
Infected fragments from right-sided vegetations lodging in the lungs, giving cavitating nodules.
Synergy
Greater killing by two drugs together than either alone — a cell-wall drug plus gentamicin for enterococci.
TTE / TOE
Transthoracic / transesophageal echocardiography (about 65% and 95% sensitive for vegetations).

Valves and valve surgery

Anitschkow cells / Aschoff bodies
Macrophages with wavy “caterpillar” chromatin / granulomatous foci of T cells, plasma cells and macrophages in rheumatic carditis.
Bioprosthetic valve
A tissue valve (porcine or bovine pericardium): aspirin only, but structural degeneration over 10–15 years.
Bicuspid aortic valve
A congenital two-cusp aortic valve in 1–2% of people; calcifies early and comes with an aortopathy.
Carcinoid heart disease
Serotonin-driven fibrous plaques on the tricuspid and pulmonic valves (TR and pulmonic stenosis).
Cardioplegia
A cold, high-potassium solution that stops the heart in diastole during surgery.
Heyde syndrome
Aortic stenosis with GI bleeding from angiodysplasia, through shear-induced loss of von Willebrand factor.
Myxomatous degeneration
Proteoglycan infiltration that makes valve leaflets long and floppy — the lesion of mitral valve prolapse.
Paravalvular leak
Regurgitation around, not through, a prosthetic valve; commonest after TAVR.
PBMC — percutaneous balloon mitral commissurotomy
A catheter balloon that splits fused commissures in rheumatic mitral stenosis.
SAVR / TAVR
Surgical (open, on bypass) / transcatheter (usually transfemoral) aortic valve replacement.
STS score
The Society of Thoracic Surgeons risk calculator for operative mortality.
Valve-in-valve
A transcatheter valve placed inside a failed bioprosthesis.

Congenital heart disease

AVSD — atrioventricular septal defect
An endocardial cushion defect involving both septa and the AV valves; the lesion of Down syndrome.
Blalock–Taussig shunt
A subclavian-to-pulmonary-artery connection that increases lung blood flow in cyanotic disease.
CCHD — critical congenital heart disease
Lesions needing intervention in the first weeks of life; screened for by newborn pulse oximetry.
Coarctation of the aorta
A juxtaductal narrowing of the aorta: arm pressure above leg pressure and weak femorals.
Ductal-dependent lesion
A defect in which systemic or pulmonary blood flow relies on an open ductus arteriosus.
Ebstein anomaly
Downward displacement of the tricuspid leaflets into the right ventricle; linked to maternal lithium.
Eisenmenger syndrome
Reversal of a long-standing left-to-right shunt after fixed pulmonary hypertension develops.
Endocardial cushions
Embryonic tissue that divides the atrioventricular canal and forms parts of the septa and AV valves.
Fixed split S2
A split second heart sound that does not vary with breathing — an ASD.
HLHS — hypoplastic left heart syndrome
A tiny left ventricle and left-sided valves; the RV supplies the body through the ductus.
Hyperoxia test
Giving 100% oxygen: lung disease improves the saturation, a cyanotic shunt does not.
Norwood, Glenn, Fontan
The three staged operations for single-ventricle hearts such as HLHS.
PGE₁ — prostaglandin E1
Keeps the ductus arteriosus open in ductal-dependent lesions.
TAPVR — total anomalous pulmonary venous return
Pulmonary veins draining to the right side of the heart.
Tet spell
A hypercyanotic episode in tetralogy of Fallot, relieved by knee-to-chest positioning or squatting.
TGA — transposition of the great arteries
The aorta arises from the RV and the pulmonary artery from the LV; survival needs mixing.
TOF — tetralogy of Fallot
VSD, RV outflow stenosis, RV hypertrophy and an overriding aorta.

Vessels

AAA — abdominal aortic aneurysm
A focal aortic dilation over 3 cm, usually infrarenal and atherosclerotic; repaired at about 5.5 cm.
ABI — ankle–brachial index
The higher ankle pressure divided by the higher arm pressure; 0.90 or less is PAD, over 1.40 means calcified arteries.
Acute limb ischemia
Sudden loss of limb perfusion (under 2 weeks), usually an embolus; the six P’s.
Aldosterone-to-renin ratio
The screening test for primary aldosteronism, done after correcting the potassium.
ANCA — antineutrophil cytoplasmic antibodies
c-ANCA (PR3) in granulomatosis with polyangiitis; p-ANCA (MPO) in microscopic polyangiitis and EGPA.
Cilostazol
A phosphodiesterase-3 inhibitor that improves walking distance in claudication; contraindicated in heart failure.
CLTI — chronic limb-threatening ischemia
Ischemic rest pain, ulcers or gangrene for 2 weeks or more; “the unstable angina of PAD.”
Compartment syndrome
Rising pressure inside a fascial compartment, here after reperfusion; pain on passive stretch; treated by fasciotomy.
Cystic medial degeneration
Loss of elastic fibres and smooth muscle with mucoid pools in the aortic media; Marfan and the dissecting aorta.
Dependent rubor
A dusky red foot when hanging down that blanches on elevation; advanced arterial insufficiency.
EVAR — endovascular aneurysm repair
A stent graft placed through the femoral arteries to exclude an aneurysm.
Familial hypercholesterolemia
An autosomal dominant LDL-receptor defect: very high LDL, tendon xanthomas, early coronary disease.
GCA — giant cell arteritis
Granulomatous large-vessel vasculitis over 50: headache, jaw claudication, high ESR, risk of blindness.
Hypertensive emergency
Severe hypertension with acute organ damage; IV drugs, lowering MAP by no more than about 25% in the first hour.
Kawasaki disease
Medium-vessel vasculitis under 5: fever 5 days or more, conjunctivitis, rash, mucosal and extremity changes; coronary aneurysms.
Leriche syndrome
Aortoiliac occlusion: buttock claudication, absent femoral pulses and erectile dysfunction.
Mesenteric ischemia
Inadequate gut blood flow: acute (embolus, thrombosis, non-occlusive, venous) or chronic (intestinal angina).
NOMI — non-occlusive mesenteric ischemia
Mesenteric vasospasm in low-flow states or on vasopressors, with patent arteries.
Neurogenic claudication
Leg pain from lumbar spinal stenosis, brought on by standing and relieved by sitting or flexing.
PCSK9 inhibitor
A monoclonal antibody (evolocumab, alirocumab) that stops LDL-receptor degradation, so the liver clears more LDL.
Primary aldosteronism
Autonomous aldosterone excess (adenoma or bilateral hyperplasia): hypertension, low potassium, metabolic alkalosis.
Stanford type A / B
Dissection involving the ascending aorta (A, surgery) or only the descending aorta (B, medical therapy).
Takayasu arteritis
Granulomatous large-vessel vasculitis of young Asian women: weak arm pulses, unequal arm pressures.
TBI — toe–brachial index
Toe pressure over arm pressure, used when ankle arteries are calcified; 0.70 or less is abnormal.
