Lipid-Lowering Drugs: Interactions and Adverse Events

Dr. Scully’s refresher worked three cases: a statin for high LDL, a fibrate for high triglycerides and low HDL after an infarct, and a PCSK9 inhibitor when nothing else reached goal. The study-guide objectives here are specific — drug–drug interactions and how to prevent adverse events — and they come down to one enzyme (CYP3A4), one protein-binding interaction (warfarin) and one combination (statin plus gemfibrozil).

Statins, ezetimibe, PCSK9 inhibitors, fibrates, niacin and the rest

Statins — first choice for high LDL

  1. A statin inhibits HMG-CoA reductase, the rate-limiting step of cholesterol synthesis.
  2. Hepatocyte cholesterol falls, and the SREBP signal switches on.
  3. LDL-receptor expression rises on hepatocytes.
  4. LDL is pulled out of the serum to replace the cell’s stores.

The step-up sequence

  1. Lifestyle and treat secondary causes (Section 30).
  2. A statin — high-intensity (atorvastatin or rosuvastatin) after an MI or with high risk.
  3. LDL still above goal: add ezetimibe (or, less often, a bile acid sequestrant).
  4. Still above goal on a maximal statin plus ezetimibe: a PCSK9 inhibitor. Patients normally must fail first-line drugs first; costs are falling.
  5. High TG or low HDL on a statin: omega-3s or a fibrate (fenofibrate if combining with a statin).

Preventing adverse events

  • Statin myopathy: it happens, but less often than first thought, and guidance now advises not front-loading counselling about it, because of the nocebo effect. Severe muscle pain with tea-coloured urine means rhabdomyolysis — stop the drug and work it up (CK, creatinine, urinalysis).
  • Board add-on Baseline lipids and LFTs; check CK only if symptoms; risk rises with age, hypothyroidism, kidney disease, high doses and interacting drugs. For intolerance: lower the dose, switch statin, alternate-day dosing, or add ezetimibe or a PCSK9 inhibitor.
  • Hepatotoxicity Board add-on: statins (and niacin, fibrates) can raise transaminases; avoid in active liver disease.
  • Niacin flushing Board add-on: take aspirin 30 minutes before, use extended-release, take with food and avoid hot drinks or alcohol at dose time.
  • Fibrate gallstones and myopathy: choose fenofibrate with a statin; avoid in kidney or liver failure.
  • Bile acid sequestrants: space other drugs; avoid if TG is over 300–500; supplement fat-soluble vitamins.
  • Pregnancy Board add-on: statins are avoided; stop before conception.

Q: How does inhibiting cholesterol synthesis lower serum LDL?

Answer

Lower hepatocyte cholesterol switches on more LDL-receptor expression, so the liver clears more LDL from the blood.

Q: A patient on simvastatin is prescribed clarithromycin and a week later has severe muscle pain and dark urine. What happened, and which two statins would have avoided it?

Answer

Clarithromycin inhibits CYP3A4, raising simvastatin levels and causing rhabdomyolysis. Pravastatin and rosuvastatin are not CYP-metabolised.

Q: A patient on warfarin is started on gemfibrozil and develops bleeding gums with a high INR. Mechanism? And which fibrate is the safer partner for a statin?

Answer

Fibrates displace warfarin from plasma-protein binding sites, raising free warfarin. With a statin, fenofibrate is safer — gemfibrozil carries the higher rhabdomyolysis risk.

Q: Why does ezetimibe lower LDL well with a statin but poorly on its own?

Answer

Blocking absorption lowers intracellular cholesterol, so the liver up-regulates its own synthesis and LDL drifts back. The statin blocks that synthesis.

Q: A 61-year-old a year after a STEMI is on simvastatin and fish oil. HDL is 32, LDL 106, triglycerides 354. What class addresses the TG and HDL, and what else would you change?

Answer

A fibrate (PPAR-α activator) lowers TG and raises HDL — fenofibrate rather than gemfibrozil alongside a statin. After an MI he should also be on a high-intensity statin (atorvastatin or rosuvastatin); an LDL of 106 is above secondary-prevention goals.

Q: LDL is still above goal on maximal atorvastatin plus ezetimibe. What comes next, and how do the two injectable forms differ?

Answer

A PCSK9 inhibitor. The monoclonal antibodies bind circulating PCSK9; inclisiran is an siRNA that reduces PCSK9 production. Both let LDL receptors recycle. The main adverse effect is injection-site reactions.

Q: A patient on cholestyramine develops easy bruising and a prolonged INR, and his levothyroxine seems to have stopped working. Explain both, and how to prevent them.

Answer

Bile acid sequestrants cause fat-soluble vitamin (K) malabsorption, and they bind other drugs in the gut. Give other medicines 1 hour before or 4 hours after the resin, and supplement vitamin K.
