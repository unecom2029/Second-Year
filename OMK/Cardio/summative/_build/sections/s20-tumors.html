Section 20 · Heart Failure & the Myocardium

Heart Tumors

Dr. Gardner’s subtitle for this topic was “only one is common.” That one is the left atrial myxoma — benign, on a stalk, and dangerous because of where it sits. The surgeon’s contribution is the reassuring part: take out the whole stalk with a cuff of septum, and it does not come back.

⚠️ “Most common” depends on the question

Both lecturers call myxoma the most common cardiac tumour. First Aid adds the distinction exams use: the most common primary tumour in adults is the myxoma, in children the rhabdomyoma, and the most common cardiac tumour overall is a metastasis. Read which one the question is asking.

🩺 Dr. McGrath’s case

A 68-year-old woman with dyspnea and a TIA; head CT negative. Transthoracic echo found a 3 cm left atrial myxoma and no coronary disease. It was resected through the right atrium together with its attachment at the fossa ovalis, and the septum patched with pericardium.

Management and long-term survival

  • Diagnosis: echocardiography (transesophageal for detail). Board add-on Cardiac MRI characterises the mass.
  • Treatment: surgical resection, promptly, because of the risk of embolism and of sudden obstruction.
  • It does not recur if you resect the whole stalk plus a piece of atrial septum, patching the septal hole with pericardium.
  • Long-term survival after complete resection is excellent — it is benign, “a tumour, not a cancer.” Board add-on Recurrence is rare (a few percent), higher in familial forms such as the Carney complex (multiple myxomas, spotty skin pigmentation, endocrine tumours), so those patients get serial echoes.
  • Metastatic disease is managed by draining the effusion (needle pericardiocentesis or a surgical subxiphoid pericardial window) and treating the primary; the outlook is that of the cancer. Surgeons may help remove a renal cell carcinoma thrombus extending up the IVC.

Q: A 50-year-old woman has weight loss, intermittent fevers, a high ESR, and dizziness that comes on when she lies down. Echo shows a pedunculated left atrial mass. Diagnosis, and which cytokine explains the systemic symptoms?

Answer

Left atrial myxoma, causing positional ball-valve obstruction of the mitral orifice. IL-6 release causes the constitutional symptoms.

Q: A woman has episodic dyspnea and near-fainting that change with position, and a TIA. Echo shows a mobile mass on a stalk attached to the fossa ovalis. How do you prevent recurrence after surgery?

Answer

Resect the whole stalk plus a cuff of atrial septum, and patch the septum. Done completely, a myxoma does not recur.

Q: A newborn with seizures and hypopigmented skin patches has several intramural masses in both ventricles on echo. What are they, and what is the associated condition?

Answer

Cardiac rhabdomyomas — the most common primary cardiac tumour in children — associated with tuberous sclerosis. Many regress on their own.

Q: A patient with metastatic lung cancer develops hypotension, distended neck veins and muffled heart sounds. How do cardiac metastases usually present, and what is the immediate treatment?

Answer

Through the pericardium, as a bloody effusion with tamponade. Drain it: pericardiocentesis, or a surgical subxiphoid pericardial window for recurrence.
