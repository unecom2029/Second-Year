Section 34 · Vessels

Vasculitis

Dr. Gardner flagged this topic: “they tend to get tested.” Most vasculitides are rare, but they mimic other diseases, are devastating if untreated, and are mostly immune-driven and highly treatable if caught early — usually with steroids first. His practical advice: in the emergency department, just thinking of vasculitis and calling the right specialist is a win. The boards will still want the details, so sort by vessel size first.

Q: A 72-year-old woman has new temporal headaches, pain when chewing, and stiffness in her shoulders and hips. Her ESR is 95. What must be started today, and why?

Answer

High-dose corticosteroids for giant cell arteritis with polymyalgia rheumatica — started before the temporal artery biopsy result, because ophthalmic artery involvement causes irreversible blindness.

Q: A 25-year-old Japanese woman has arm claudication, unequal arm blood pressures and an absent radial pulse. Diagnosis, vessels involved, and the confirming test?

Answer

Takayasu arteritis (“pulseless disease”) — granulomatous inflammation of the aortic arch and its major branches; confirm with angiography (CT angiography or DSA). Treat with corticosteroids.

Q: A 2-year-old has had fever for 8 days, red eyes, cracked lips, a neck node and a rash; platelets are 960K. What is the most important test, and the treatment?

Answer

Kawasaki disease. An urgent echocardiogram for coronary artery aneurysms, and IVIG plus aspirin immediately.

Q: A 45-year-old has chronic bloody sinusitis, cavitating lung nodules and red-cell casts in the urine. Which antibody, which histology, and which mechanism?

Answer

Granulomatosis with polyangiitis: c-ANCA / anti-PR3; necrotising granulomas with small-vessel vasculitis in the upper airway, lung and kidney. The mechanism is ANCA activation of primed neutrophils (pauci-immune).

Q: A 50-year-old with chronic hepatitis B has hypertension, abdominal pain after meals, foot drop and a raised creatinine. His chest X-ray is clear and ANCA is negative. Diagnosis and the histology?

Answer

Polyarteritis nodosa — a medium-vessel, immune-complex vasculitis linked to hepatitis B, sparing the lung, ANCA-negative. Histology: transmural fibrinoid necrosis of medium arteries, with lesions of different ages.

Q: A 7-year-old, two weeks after a cold, has palpable purpura on the buttocks and legs, knee pain, colicky abdominal pain and hematuria. What deposits in the vessel walls, and what is the mechanism?

Answer

IgA immune complexes — IgA vasculitis (Henoch–Schönlein purpura), a leukocytoclastic small-vessel vasculitis: immune complexes deposit, fix complement and draw in neutrophils.

Q: An asthmatic adult develops foot drop, purpura and an eosinophil count of 20%. Diagnosis? And a 35-year-old two-pack-a-day smoker has a fingertip ulcer, normal radial pulses and corkscrew collaterals on a hand angiogram — diagnosis and treatment?

Answer

The first is eosinophilic granulomatosis with polyangiitis (Churg–Strauss) — often p-ANCA, about half ANCA-negative. The second is thromboangiitis obliterans (Buerger disease); the only treatment that works is complete smoking cessation.
