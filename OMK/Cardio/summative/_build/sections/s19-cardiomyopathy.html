The Cardiomyopathies

A cardiomyopathy is a disease of the heart muscle itself. Dr. Gardner sorted them by shape — dilated, hypertrophic, restrictive — and by what the shape does: a dilated heart cannot pump (systolic failure), a hypertrophic or restrictive heart cannot fill (diastolic failure). Learn each one as a shape, a cause list, an echo picture, a slide and a treatment.

Hypertrophic cardiomyopathy — sudden death in young athletes

  • Genetics: autosomal dominant, males more than females; sarcomere mutations — MYBPC3, MYH7 (β-myosin heavy chain), troponin I and T.
  • Two problems at once: the thick wall limits diastolic filling, and the hypertrophied septum interferes with mitral closure, so some blood regurgitates to the left atrium. In HOCM the septal bulge plus systolic anterior motion of the mitral leaflet obstructs the outflow tract.
  • Exam: a harsh crescendo–decrescendo systolic murmur that gets louder with Valsalva (anything that shrinks the ventricle) and softer with squatting or handgrip — worked through in Section 3. Board add-on An S4.
  • The hypertrophy usually does not appear until early adolescence, so screen first-degree relatives repeatedly.
  • How it kills: VT or VF, probably from transient hypoxia in scarred, thick muscle. HCM is the commonest cause of sudden death in young athletes — about 36% of 1,435 cases, ahead of coronary artery anomalies (17%). Basketball is the sport most often involved.
  • Sudden-death risk markers: family history of sudden death, unexplained syncope, severe LV hypertrophy, non-sustained VT, an abnormal blood-pressure response to exercise, outflow obstruction, fibrosis on MRI.
🩺 The championship-game case

A 16-year-old soccer player had three months of palpitations and dizziness after sprints that he told no one about, then a non-contact collapse. The AED read VT and needed two shocks. ECG: sinus rhythm with LV hypertrophy. Bedside echo: a massively thickened septum. “AEDs are life-saving and should be around everywhere, especially at sporting events” — HCM mortality has fallen from 3–6% to about 1% because of them.

Other cardiomyopathies worth knowing Board add-on

  • Takotsubo (stress) cardiomyopathy: a postmenopausal woman after an emotional or physical stressor, chest pain, ST changes and a modest troponin rise, with apical ballooning and clean coronaries. Probably a catecholamine surge. Recovers over weeks (Section 37 lists it among the causes that cluster in women).
  • Peripartum cardiomyopathy: dilated, in the last month of pregnancy or the months after delivery.
  • Chagas disease: a dilated cardiomyopathy with right bundle branch block decades after Trypanosoma cruzi infection, now considered endemic to the United States (Section 23).

“Cobalt-beer cardiomyopathy”: in 1964–66, cobalt salts added to canned beer to stabilise the foam caused a specific cardiomyopathy in heavy drinkers.

Q: A 17-year-old collapses and dies during a basketball game. His father died suddenly at 35. At autopsy there is asymmetric septal hypertrophy. What will histology show, which gene family is mutated, and how is it inherited?

Answer

Myofibre disarray of hypertrophied myocytes. Sarcomere genes — β-myosin heavy chain (MYH7), myosin-binding protein C (MYBPC3), troponins. Autosomal dominant. This is hypertrophic cardiomyopathy, the commonest cause of sudden death in young athletes.

Q: A woman treated for breast cancer with doxorubicin develops dyspnea two years later. Echo shows a dilated left ventricle with an EF of 25%. Classify the cardiomyopathy, and how is the risk limited?

Answer

Dilated cardiomyopathy with systolic dysfunction from anthracycline cardiotoxicity, which is dose-dependent — hence lifetime dose limits.

Q: Which cardiomyopathy pattern does cardiac amyloidosis produce, what is its pericardial mimic, and what ECG finding is the giveaway?

Answer

Restrictive — diastolic dysfunction with a preserved EF and big atria. The mimic is constrictive pericarditis. The ECG clue is low voltage despite a thick myocardium.

Q: A patient with known HCM becomes lightheaded after being started on a diuretic and a nitrate for “heart failure.” His systolic murmur is louder. Why, and what should he be on instead?

Answer

Lowering preload shrinks the ventricle, bringing the septum and mitral leaflet closer and worsening the outflow obstruction. Stop both; use a beta-blocker (or a non-dihydropyridine calcium channel blocker) to slow the rate and lengthen filling.

Q: Why might a 9-year-old who carries his family’s HCM mutation have a normal echocardiogram?

Answer

The hypertrophy usually does not appear until early adolescence. First-degree relatives need repeated screening through the teenage years.

Q: A young cyclist has exertional palpitations and a VT with left bundle branch block morphology. MRI shows a thin, poorly contracting right ventricle infiltrated by fat. Diagnosis?

Answer

Arrhythmogenic right ventricular cardiomyopathy — inherited fibro-fatty replacement of the right ventricle. Stop endurance sport; ICD for high risk.
