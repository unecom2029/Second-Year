Cardiac Biomarkers: Troponin, BNP and NT-proBNP

Two blood tests answer two different questions. Troponin asks “are heart muscle cells dying right now?” The natriuretic peptides ask “is the heart wall being stretched?” Most exam traps come from forgetting which question a test answers, or from the things that push each test up or down without heart disease.

Troponin: physiology, timing, sensitivity and specificity ★ HY

Where it comes from

  • The troponin complex sits on the thin filament with tropomyosin. TnC binds calcium, TnI inhibits actin–myosin binding at rest, TnT anchors the complex to tropomyosin (Section 1 has the contraction mechanics).
  • The cardiac isoforms of TnI and TnT differ from skeletal muscle ones, which is what makes the test heart-specific.
  • Dying myocytes release troponin, and the size of the rise tracks the amount of injury. STEMIs peak far higher than NSTEMIs.

How it is used

  • Myocardial injury means a troponin above the 99th percentile of the upper reference limit. Acute injury means a rise and/or fall on serial values.
  • With high-sensitivity assays most patients are ruled in or out on the first value; equivocal ones are repeated at 1 or 3 hours, and the rate of change is diagnostic. Dr. Watkins’s patient went from 50 to 325 in four hours.
  • Infarction = acute injury plus a clinical picture of ischemia (symptoms, ECG changes, a new wall-motion abnormality).
  • Troponin is an emergency-department test. If you are worried enough to order one in the office, the patient belongs in the ED.

Sensitivity, specificity and weaknesses

  • More sensitive and more specific than CK-MB for myocardial necrosis — very specific for heart muscle, but not specific for infarction.
  • It rises with any myocyte injury. The lecture’s list: a marathon, pulmonary embolism, myocarditis and chronic heart failure — without the steep rise or the high peak of an infarct.
  • Board add-on Others: sepsis and type 2 (supply–demand) infarction, tachyarrhythmias, takotsubo cardiomyopathy, cardioversion or cardiac contusion, stroke, and chronic kidney disease (reduced clearance gives a chronically raised, flat level).
  • It cannot detect injury in the first hour or so, and a single value says nothing about direction — hence serial testing.
  • A flat, mildly raised troponin that does not change on repeat means chronic injury or a secondary (type 2) event, not plaque rupture — the cocaine heart-failure case in Application Session 2.

BNP versus NT-proBNP: physiology and limitations ★ HY

Physiology

  • Ventricular myocytes respond to wall stretch — volume or pressure overload — by making pro-BNP, which is cleaved into two pieces in equal amounts: active BNP (32 amino acids) and inert NT-proBNP (76 amino acids).
  • Board add-on Its sibling ANP comes from the atria in response to atrial stretch. Both act through cGMP.
  • What BNP does: vasodilation, natriuresis and diuresis (it dilates the afferent arteriole and raises the glomerular filtration rate), and it suppresses renin, aldosterone and sympathetic tone. It is the body’s counter-regulator to the renin–angiotensin system in heart failure — a fight it eventually loses (Section 17).
  • BNP is cleared by neprilysin and by natriuretic-peptide clearance receptors. NT-proBNP is cleared mainly by the kidney, which is why it lasts longer.

How it is used

  • Diagnostic and prognostic in acute and chronic heart failure. Its strength is the negative predictive value: in a breathless patient, a low level makes heart failure very unlikely.
  • That makes it the tiebreaker between heart failure and pneumonia or a COPD exacerbation.
  • BNP also carries prognostic value in asymptomatic or minimally symptomatic left ventricular dysfunction.

🧠 Telling them apart in one line

Troponin is a crime-scene marker — it leaks out of cells that have already been damaged. BNP is a stress signal — a hormone the stretched ventricle chooses to secrete. NT-proBNP is BNP’s receipt: made in the same amount, does nothing, and hangs around longer.

Q: A patient with heart failure with reduced ejection fraction is switched from enalapril to sacubitril/valsartan. Three months later he feels much better, but his BNP has risen. Which test should you follow instead, and why?

Answer

NT-proBNP. Sacubitril inhibits neprilysin, the enzyme that degrades BNP, so BNP rises as a drug effect. NT-proBNP is an inert fragment that neprilysin does not clear, so it still reflects wall stretch.

Q: A healthy 35-year-old runner has a mildly raised troponin the morning after a marathon. How does his troponin curve differ from that of an infarction?

Answer

It is a low, flat, short-lived bump with no steep rise and no high peak. Serial testing showing the rate of change separates it from an infarction, along with the absence of an ischemic story or ECG changes.

Q: A patient had an NSTEMI 4 days ago. Today he has new chest pain and ST changes. His troponin is still elevated from the first event. Which marker is classically used to diagnose re-infarction, and why?

Answer

CK-MB, because it returns to normal in 2–3 days while troponin stays up for 7–10 days. A new CK-MB rise points to a new event. (In practice a fresh rise on serial high-sensitivity troponin is now used.)

Q: A 68-year-old with a body-mass index of 44 presents with dyspnea, orthopnea and leg edema. Her BNP is 60 pg/mL. Does this rule out heart failure?

Answer

No. Natriuretic peptides are falsely low in obesity because adipose tissue clears them faster. With a convincing clinical picture, get an echocardiogram rather than relying on the number.

Q: Which natriuretic peptide is biologically active, which has the longer half-life, and which rises more in chronic kidney disease?

Answer

BNP is active (vasodilation, natriuresis, renin suppression). NT-proBNP has the longer half-life (1–2 hours versus about 20 minutes) and rises more in kidney disease, because it is cleared by the kidney.
