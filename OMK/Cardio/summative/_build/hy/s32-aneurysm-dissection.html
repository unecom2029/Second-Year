Aneurysms & Dissection

  • True (saccular / fusiform, all layers) · false (breach + hematoma) · dissection (intimal tear → blood in media → false lumen).
  • Causes: weak material (Marfan, EDS), remodelling (MMPs), vasa vasorum ischemia, infection (syphilis → thoracic, tree-bark; mycotic). Thoracic: cystic medial degeneration. AAA: transmural inflammation, mural thrombus.
  • AAA: commonest, infrarenal, smoking/age/male/family; rupture risk ↑ female, HTN, COPD, smoking. Screen men 65–75 who ever smoked (one US). CT gold standard.
  • Chart: <4.5 US q6mo · 4.5–5.0 q3mo · 5.0–5.5 repair if female, familial, rapid growth · >5.5 repair. Also symptomatic, >5 mm/6 mo, saccular.
  • EVAR (neck >15 mm) vs open. Post-op bloody diarrhea = ischemic colitis (IMA). Graft infection → remove graft. Aortoenteric fistula → herald bleed.
  • Splenic aneurysm ruptures in pregnancy (>2 cm repair). Popliteal: embolises, often bilateral + AAA.
  • Dissection: HTN, Marfan/EDS/LDS/Turner, BAV, cocaine, pregnancy; tearing pain to the back, arm BP difference, widened mediastinum; can present as stroke.
  • Stanford A (ascending; DeBakey I, II) → emergency surgery. Stanford B (distal to L subclavian; DeBakey III) → IV β-blocker first (HR ~60, SBP 100–120), then vasodilator; TEVAR if complicated. Adamkiewicz → paraplegia; CSF drain.
