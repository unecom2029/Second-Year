ECG Patterns of Disease

  • Atria: RAE = tall peaked P in II (>2.5 mm, "P pulmonale"), mostly positive V1 · LAE = wide notched P (>120 ms, "P mitrale"), mostly negative V1. RA makes the P taller, LA makes it wider.
  • LVH: left axis + R in aVL >11 mm (most specific) or S(V1/V2) + R(V5/V6) ≥35 mm (most sensitive). Strain = TWI/ST depression, mimics ischemia.
  • RVH: right axis + reversed R-wave progression (tall R in V1). Normal transition R=S at V3–V4.
  • LBBB: QRS ≥120, left axis, V1–V3 negative, broad notched R in I/aVL/V5–V6 · W in V1, M in V6 (WiLLiaM).
  • RBBB: QRS ≥120, normal axis, rSR′ rabbit ears V1–V2, slurred S in I/V5–V6 · M in V1, W in V6 (MaRRoW).
  • Fascicular block = normal QRS width, axis shift with no other cause. Bundle = wide QRS.
  • Territories: II, III, aVF = RCA (inferior) · V1–V3 anteroseptal, V4–V6 anterior = LAD · I, aVL, V5–V6 = LCx/diagonal. LCx has no direct leads → reciprocal change in V1–V3.
  • ST elevation = transmural = STEMI (≥1 mm, 2 contiguous leads) → open the artery now. ST depression = subendocardial = NSTEMI → stabilise, no emergent cath. Q waves = old, dead (normal only in III). TWI = non-specific.
  • Reciprocal ST depression confirms a STEMI. Evolution: hyperacute T → STE → Q + TWI. Posterior MI = ST depression + tall R + upright T in V1–V3.
  • Hyperkalemia: tall narrow peaked T, then everything widens. Hypokalemia: flat T + U wave → long-looking QT → torsades.
  • QT: eyeball test — T ends before halfway between R waves = normal. Lengtheners: dofetilide/sotalol, low K⁺/Mg²⁺, fluoroquinolones, antidepressants, antiemetics, antifungals, congenital LQTS.
