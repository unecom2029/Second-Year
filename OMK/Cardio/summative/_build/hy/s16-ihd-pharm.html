Antianginal Drugs

  • Lower demand — the easy lever. Stable: SL NTG + β-blocker (best monotherapy, resting HR ~50) ± CCB/long nitrate → ranolazine. Vasospastic: SL NTG + CCB, no β-blocker (unopposed α).
  • Nitrates: ALDH2 → NO → sGC → cGMP → venodilation, ↓ preload. Headache, flushing, hypotension, reflex tachycardia. Tolerance → nitrate-free interval overnight. ISMN = high oral bioavailability. ALDH2 polymorphism (East Asian) → less effect.
  • PDE5 inhibitor + nitrate = refractory hypotension. Also avoid nitrates in RV infarct, HOCM, severe AS, hypotension.
  • DHP CCB: ↓ afterload, edema, reflex tachycardia. Verapamil/diltiazem: ↓ HR + contractility, AV block, constipation. β-blocker + IV verapamil contraindicated.
  • Ranolazine: blocks late Na⁺ current → less Ca²⁺ overload, ↓ diastolic wall stress; no HR/BP change; QT ↑, dizziness, constipation; women benefit more.
  • Ivabradine = funny current (If/HCN), not late Na⁺ (slide slip).
  • ACS bundle: aspirin, P2Y12 inhibitor, anticoagulant, NTG, β-blocker (not in shock/bradycardia/ADHF/cocaine), high-intensity statin, O₂ only if hypoxic, reperfusion.
