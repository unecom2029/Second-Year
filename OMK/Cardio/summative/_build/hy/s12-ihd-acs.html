Stable Angina vs ACS

  • Stable: fixed ≥70% stenosis, exertional, minutes, rest relieves, troponin normal. UA: rupture + non-occlusive clot, crescendo or rest, troponin normal. NSTEMI: partial occlusion, subendocardial, troponin ↑, ST↓/T inversion. STEMI: complete occlusion, transmural, ST↑ → Q waves.
  • Vasospastic: young, rest/night, transient ST↑, no plaque needed.
  • Flow reserve: stress flow falls at ~50% stenosis, rest flow at ~80–90%.
  • Type 1 (primary) = plaque rupture, younger men · Type 2 (secondary) = supply–demand (sepsis, tachycardia, anemia), older women · MINOCA 10–15%.
  • History: pressure, substernal, radiating, minutes, exertional, inducible ("can you make it happen?"). Women, diabetics, elderly atypical. NTG relief not diagnostic.
  • Testing: pre-test probability first. Exercise + readable ECG → ETT (± imaging); unreadable ECG → exercise imaging; can't exercise → pharmacologic stress or CCTA. Never stress-test active ischemia. Stress echo better in women.
  • Cath timing: STEMI door-to-balloon <90 min · NSTE-ACS very high risk (shock, refractory pain, arrhythmia) <2 h · high risk (troponin+) <24 h · intermediate <72 h.
  • ACS in the office: aspirin, NTG, O₂ if hypoxic, monitor, ambulance — never by car. Troponin = ED only.
  • Optimal medical therapy: antiplatelet + statin + ACEi/ARB + β-blocker (~13% vs ~20% 10-yr mortality).
