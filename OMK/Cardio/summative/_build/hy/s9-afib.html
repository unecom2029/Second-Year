Atrial Fibrillation

  • Irregularly irregular, no P waves, no isoelectric baseline, usually narrow. No two "P waves" look the same.
  • Paroxysmal ≤7 days · persistent >7 days and needed intervention · long-standing persistent >12 months · permanent = jointly decided to stop trying.
  • Young: pulmonary-vein triggered, autonomic/genetic. Old: remodelled scarred "maze" — no complete cure by ablation.
  • Triggers: hypertension, coronary disease, valve disease, low K⁺/Mg²⁺, infection, thyrotoxicosis, alcohol ("holiday heart", #3 risk factor), PE, sympathomimetics, obesity, sleep apnea.
  • Most common symptoms = fatigue and shortness of breath, not palpitations (only ~50–60% feel those).
  • Steps: diagnose → how long? → anticoagulation score → rate control first → treat the cause.
  • CHA₂DS₂-VASc: CHF 1 · HTN 1 · Age ≥75 = 2 · DM 1 · Stroke/TIA = 2 · Vascular 1 · Age 65–74 1 · female 1. ≥2 → anticoagulate (usually a DOAC). Female sex is a modifier and is dropped in CHA₂DS₂-VA.
  • Flutter is anticoagulated exactly like AF — same score, class 1 recommendation.
  • AFFIRM found rate and rhythm control equivalent; today rhythm control is favoured long term for symptomatic patients or reduced function. Rate control: β-blocker or verapamil/diltiazem. Rhythm control: class I or III.
  • Two dangers: stroke and heart failure. Losing the atrial kick costs ~15% of filling (physiology lecture says ~10% at rest); a persistently fast rate causes tachycardia-mediated cardiomyopathy.
