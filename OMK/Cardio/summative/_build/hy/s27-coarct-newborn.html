Coarctation & the Newborn Murmur

  • Check femorals on every newborn. Coarct: weak/delayed femorals, arm BP > leg (≥20 mm Hg), murmur to the back, cool mottled legs; juxtaductal; boys 2:1; Turner (25–30%), BAV.
  • Critical coarct is ductal-dependent → shock on day 1–2 → PGE₁, then balloon or resection by gradient. Late: arm HTN, rib notching, berry aneurysms.
  • Incidence: VSD > PDA > PS > ASD > coarct > BAV > TOF > TGA (lecture chart); BAV now ≈ VSD with routine echo.
  • Newborn murmur: well baby → watch (closing PDA/PFO); persists or louder → refer, ECG + echo (definitive). Diastolic, harsh, thrill, click, abnormal S2, symptoms → echo. Roughly a third to half of referred newborn murmurs are structural (approximate; not in lecture).
  • No murmur ≠ no disease: pulse-ox screening after 24 h (hand + foot), <95% or a difference → echo.
