Antiarrhythmic Drugs

  • Rate = nodal tissue = classes II and IV. Rhythm = myocytes = classes I and III.
  • Phase 0 is Ca²⁺ in the node and Na⁺ in the myocyte; K⁺ repolarises both. Funny current (Na⁺) drifts phase 4 and is opened by hyperpolarisation.
  • I = fast Na⁺ block, ↓ phase 0 slope, wider QRS · II = β-block, flatter phase 4, longer PR · III = K⁺ block, longer phase 2–3 and ERP, longer QT · IV = L-type Ca²⁺ block, slower nodal phase 0, longer PR.
  • Phase 0 slowing: C > A > B. Repolarisation Goldilocks: IA prolongs, IB shortens, IC unchanged. IA therefore carries torsades risk, like class III.
  • Use-dependent block: class I binds open/inactivated channels, so faster rates and ischemic tissue get more block.
  • IA: quinidine (cinchonism: tinnitus, blurred vision, delirium; raises digoxin), procainamide (drug-induced lupus; drug of choice in WPW + AF; no digoxin interaction), disopyramide (most anticholinergic).
  • IB: lidocaine (IV, ventricular, CNS effects and seizures), mexiletine (oral). IB is Best post-MI; IC is Contraindicated.
  • IC: flecainide, propafenone — AF prevention in a structurally normal heart only. Widen the QRS, more with exertion. Always pair with an AV-nodal blocker or you get 1:1 flutter.
  • II: 1st gen non-selective propranolol (not in asthma) · 2nd gen β1-selective metoprolol, esmolol (very short half-life, unstable patients) · 3rd gen carvedilol/labetalol (+α1), nebivolol (NO). Propranolol for a long QT (no repolarisation effect); labetalol in pregnancy (α1 block protects placental perfusion). Lipophilic drugs → vivid nightmares.
  • III: ibutilide (IV, terminates only, TdP ~2%) · dofetilide (oral, terminates and maintains, no negative inotropy so OK with low EF, renally dosed) · sotalol (II + III). Started in hospital.
  • Amiodarone = all four classes. Toxicities: pulmonary fibrosis (baseline + annual PFTs), thyroid (37% iodine; TFTs), liver, neuropathy, corneal microdeposits, photosensitivity and blue-grey skin. Safe in HFrEF; low actual torsades risk.
  • IV: verapamil (heart) → diltiazem (both) → "-dipines" (vessels). IV verapamil + β-blocker = electromechanical dissociation, contraindicated. Verapamil → constipation; diltiazem → edema.
  • Adenosine: P1 receptor → Gi/o → opens K⁺ channels → hyperpolarises the node. 6 then 12 mg, hard push + flush. Doom, flushing, chest pain, bronchospasm up to 30 min in asthma. Theophylline and caffeine block it.
  • Digoxin: blocks Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase → ↑ intracellular Na⁺ → NCX can't extrude Ca²⁺ → ↑ SR Ca²⁺ → ↑ contractility; plus ↑ vagal tone and slower AV conduction. Hypokalemia increases toxicity — check K⁺ first. Scooped "reverse check" ST depression, yellow-green halos, anorexia. Antidote: fix K⁺, digoxin immune Fab. Interactions: loop/thiazide diuretics, erythromycin (kills gut flora), quinidine, verapamil/diltiazem.
  • Atropine: muscarinic antagonist, crosses the blood–brain barrier. First-line for symptomatic bradycardia and AV block; catecholamines second. Toxidrome → physostigmine (crosses the BBB; pyridostigmine doesn't; pralidoxime is for organophosphates).
