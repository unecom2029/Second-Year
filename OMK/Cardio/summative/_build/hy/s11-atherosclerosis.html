Arteriosclerosis vs Atherosclerosis

  • Arteriosclerosis = any hardening (umbrella); atherosclerosis = the intimal-plaque subset.
  • Hyaline arteriolosclerosis: glassy pink wall, benign HTN/diabetes → nephrosclerosis. Hyperplastic: "onion skin" ± fibrinoid necrosis, malignant HTN.
  • Mönckeberg: calcium in the media, lumen spared, asymptomatic, pipe-stem on X-ray. Intimal hyperplasia: after stents/grafts → restenosis. Don't confuse with FMD (string of beads, young women's renal arteries).
  • Injury response: SMCs cross the IEL into the intima, proliferate, lay down matrix → intimal thickening → stenosis. Turbulent flow at branch points.
  • Plaque: injury → LDL in, oxidised → monocyte → macrophage → foam cell (fatty streak) → platelets/PDGF → SMC migration (fibrofatty atheroma) → necrotic core, cholesterol clefts, calcium. All inside the intima.
  • Sites: abdominal aorta > coronaries > popliteal > carotid > circle of Willis.
  • Thin cap = vulnerable → rupture/hemorrhage → thrombosis → MI, sudden death. Thick cap/fibrocalcific = stable → critical stenosis → angina.
  • Risk: LDL (main driver), smoking (initial injury), HTN, diabetes (glycation), age, male, family history. Risk factors multiply.
