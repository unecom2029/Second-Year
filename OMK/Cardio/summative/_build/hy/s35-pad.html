Peripheral arterial disease

  • Vascular claudication: cramping calf, fixed distance, relieved by standing still in minutes, weak pulses · neurogenic: standing triggers it, relieved by sitting/flexing (shopping cart, better uphill), normal pulses.
  • Smoking (risk persists ~30 yr after quitting) + diabetes (limb-threatening, tibial). Most PAD is silent or atypical.
  • ABI = higher ankle ÷ higher arm: ≤0.90 PAD · 0.91–0.99 borderline · >1.40 calcified → toe–brachial index · ≤0.40 rest pain/tissue loss.
  • CTA diagnoses; angiogram is for procedures. Dependent rubor fades with elevation (cellulitis doesn't). Blue toe = look upstream.
  • Leriche: buttock claudication + absent femoral pulses + ED (aortoiliac).
  • Acute limb ischemia (6 P's, AF embolus): IV heparin first → lysis if motor/sensory intact, else Fogarty embolectomy; reperfusion compartment syndrome → fasciotomy.
  • Rx: supervised exercise (30–45 min, ≥3×/wk, ≥12 wk), quit smoking, high-intensity statin, SAPT or aspirin + rivaroxaban 2.5 bid (↓ MACE + MALE); cilostazol (PDE3) ↑ walking, contraindicated in HF. Revascularise for lifestyle-limiting symptoms, rest pain, tissue loss.
  • Claudication: limb loss rare (~1%/yr); MI/stroke/death far likelier.
