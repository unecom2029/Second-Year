Endocarditis: Diagnosis & Treatment

  • Fever 90%, new murmur 85% (MR, AR, TR). Splenomegaly, petechiae ~40%.
  • Emboli: Janeway (painless palms/soles), splinters, petechiae. Immune complex: Osler (painful pads — "Ouch"), Roth spots, GN, +RF (50–60%).
  • 3 blood-culture sets before antibiotics (low-level continuous bacteremia). TTE ~65% → TOE ~95%; CT for abscess; PET for prosthetics.
  • Duke 2023 definite: 2 major · 1 major + 3 minor · 5 minor. Possible: 1 + 1 or 3 minor. Majors: typical organism ×2 sets, vegetation/abscess/new regurgitation.
  • Bactericidal, 4–6 weeks. Empiric: vancomycin + cefepime/ceftriaxone. MSSA: nafcillin/oxacillin (~6 wk; handout "3–5 days" typo). MRSA & S. epidermidis: vanc + rifampin (biofilm). Viridans: PCN G or ceftriaxone 4 wk. Enterococci: ampicillin/PCN + gentamicin (synergy). HACEK: ceftriaxone. Bartonella: doxy + rifampin.
  • Mechanisms: β-lactams → PBPs; vancomycin → D-Ala-D-Ala; aminoglycosides → 30S; rifampin → RNA polymerase.
  • Surgery: HF from valve damage, abscess/heart block, persistent bacteremia, fungi/resistant organisms, large vegetation with emboli, prosthetic dehiscence.
  • Prophylaxis (amoxicillin) only for prosthetic valve, prior IE, certain CHD, transplant valvulopathy.
