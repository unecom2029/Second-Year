Aortic Valve Replacement

  • Indications: severe AS + any symptom, severe AS + EF <50%, severe AS at other cardiac surgery (severe = ≥4 m/s, ≥40 mm Hg, ≤1.0 cm²). AR: symptoms, EF <50%, ESD >55 mm.
  • Cath before SAVR (~50% need CABG too). STS risk score.
  • SAVR: sternotomy + bypass + cardioplegia; younger, mechanical option, root/CABG at the same time; mortality <1% low risk; 4–6 weeks recovery.
  • TAVR: transfemoral, balloon valve during rapid pacing; elderly/high risk, now first-line for most AS; ~2.5% mortality; needs a calcified annulus → not for pure AR.
  • Mechanical: lasts forever, lifelong warfarin (no DOACs), heparin bridge. Tissue: aspirin only, wears out (~90% free of redo at 15 yr aortic) → valve-in-valve. Tissue for older patients and women planning pregnancy.
  • Complications: heart block → pacemaker (SAVR 3–4%, more with TAVR), RCA ostium obstruction, paravalvular leak (TAVR), stroke, vascular injury, valve thrombosis (mechanical, low INR), hemolysis (schistocytes), structural degeneration (tissue), prosthetic-valve endocarditis (S. epidermidis early).
