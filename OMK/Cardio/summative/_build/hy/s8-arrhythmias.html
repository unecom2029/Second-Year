Arrhythmias & Heart Block

  • Mechanisms: automaticity · triggered (EAD → long QT → torsades; DAD → ischemia, digoxin) · re-entry = most common. Slow path = short refractory; fast path = long refractory; a premature beat blocks the fast path, goes down slow, returns up fast.
  • AV block — measure P–P, R–R, PR. 1st: PR long but constant. Mobitz I: PR lengthens then drops; PR after the drop is shorter than before it; benign. Mobitz II: PR constant, sudden drop; pacemaker. 3rd: P and QRS both regular but dissociated.
  • P–P is constant in every AV block. If the Ps don't march, it isn't block.
  • 2:1 block: never two conducted Ps in a row → cannot subtype. Let the history decide; look for the P hidden in the T.
  • SVT is a category, not a diagnosis. Sinus tach = most common. AVNRT = down slow / up fast → atria and ventricles fire together → P hidden in the QRS.
  • Breaking SVT: unstable → sedate + synchronised cardioversion. Stable → vagal (carotid sinus 10–20 s) → adenosine 6 then 12 mg rapid push + flush → IV β-blocker/diltiazem/verapamil → cardioversion. Long term: EP study + ablation (class I).
  • Adenosine: half-life <5 s; flushing, chest pain, doom, brief asystole, bronchospasm. Avoid in heart transplant and WPW.
  • Carotid massage contraindications: recent MI; stroke/TIA <3 months; carotid occlusion; VT/VF history; prior bad reaction. Bruit = image first.
  • Typical flutter: negative sawtooth II, III, aVF; positive V1; counterclockwise around the tricuspid annulus via the cavotricuspid isthmus. Atria 250–300 → locks 2:1 at ~150. Adenosine unmasks, doesn't break it. CTI ablation; ~50% later get AF.
  • Wide + can't tell → treat as VT. Favours VT: AV dissociation (25–33%), capture/fusion beats (~5%, nearly diagnostic), negative precordial concordance (virtually diagnostic), northwest axis, QRS >140/160, slurred from the first millisecond. SVT + BBB has a fast initial deflection.
  • Brugada steps: no RS in any precordial lead → R-to-S >100 ms (onset of R to nadir of S) → AV dissociation → morphology. First yes = VT.
  • WPW: no adenosine, no CCB, no β-blocker, no vagal manoeuvres. Give procainamide, or sedate and cardiovert. Short PR + delta wave; pattern = no symptoms, syndrome = + arrhythmia. Ebstein anomaly is the associated lesion.
  • Congenital long QT: 1 in 5,000, autosomal dominant, mostly K⁺ channels, LQT1–3 = 85–90%. Syncope and sudden death. Acute torsades: IV magnesium.
  • Genetic syndromes: LQTS · short QT · WPW · Brugada (coved STE V1–V2, RV outflow tract) · ARVC (fibro-fatty RV) · HCM, the most common.
