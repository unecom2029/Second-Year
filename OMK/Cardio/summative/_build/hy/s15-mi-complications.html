Post-MI Complications

  • 0–24 h: VF/VT (sudden death), bradycardia/AV block (RCA → SA 60%, AV 90%), acute HF, cardiogenic shock (≥40% LV).
  • 1–3 d: fibrinous pericarditis.
  • 3–14 d (soft wall): papillary rupture — inferior MI, posteromedial (PDA only), flash pulmonary edema, apical murmur to axilla (may be silent) · septal rupture — holosystolic LSB murmur + thrill, O₂ step-up RA→PA · free wall — anterior, elderly woman, first MI, tamponade, PEA, no murmur · pseudoaneurysm (pericardium + clot, operate).
  • Weeks–months: true aneurysm (persistent ST↑ + Q, dyskinetic, mural thrombus, VT), Dressler (>2 wk, autoimmune pericarditis), chronic HF.
  • RV infarct: hypotension + JVD + clear lungs → fluids, no nitrates.
  • Free-wall timing differs: lecture 3–7 d, Watkins 24 h + 3–5 d, First Aid 5–14 d.
  • Mortality ~30% year 1, then ~3%/yr. AKI in ~40% of CPR survivors.
